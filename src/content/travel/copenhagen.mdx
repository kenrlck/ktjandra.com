Denmark was the first country I visited in Europe. I went during the 2026 World Cup, and with a much better soccer culture than the US, Copenhagen was lively. It was also a sunny summer, so it seemed like everyone was having a good time outdoors.

If I had to put Copenhagen in a few words: hygge, fast bikes, canals, tall people, baked goods (and good food in general), cigarettes, headphones, an accent with a hiccup in it, and shops tucked below street level. Hygge is the Danish word for a cozy, content feeling, usually with good company. The hiccup is the stød, a little catch in the throat that Danish puts in the middle of words.

My hotel was close to Central Station, where the train from the airport drops you off, right next to Tivoli. I didn't make it into Tivoli with my limited time, but it's on the list for next time. The jet lag had me up early every morning. The city felt walkable to me, though I may not have gone far enough outside the main spots to really test that.

## The water

The canals were first on my list. In the morning they're quiet. People are out exercising or on their way somewhere, headphones in, keeping to themselves, and they walk and bike fast.

<Photo
  src="/copenhagen/04-moored-boat.jpg"
  alt="A small white motorboat moored at a grassy bank, with a yellow building and trees behind"
  caption="A quiet stretch of canal."
/>

Boats of every size come and go, and riding them is popular. Parts of the harbor are partitioned off for the water: platforms to jump off, an area for water polo, spots to swim. The boats are also a normal way to commute, and getting from one part of the city to another by water works well (with great views).

<Photo
  src="/copenhagen/05-harbor-boats.jpg"

Some of the docked boats have been converted into apartments, restaurants and other mixed-use spaces, which I find very neat.

I took the boat tour, and I'd recommend it. You get a sense of the neighborhoods, find the ones you like, and go back to explore them on foot. I made a note of Refshaleøen, which has the Urban Rigger apartments. According to the tour guide, they're some of the most premium real estate in Copenhagen. Real estate in the city in general is very expensive, and it's getting out of reach for locals. People on land wave at the boats a lot, which I found interesting.

## On land

People park their bikes in the rack as is. Nobody chains them to anything, and you don't see the half-stripped frames missing a wheel that you see in San Francisco. The racks are almost always full, so they're clearly not there for decoration.

On my second day I caught the changing of the guard at Amalienborg, the royal family's residence: four matching palaces around one square. The guards march over from Rosenborg Castle every day, and people crowd along that path. A few people climbed the fence for a better view and got told off quickly. The ceremony changes depending on whether the King is in residence, and when he isn't, there's no band.

## Eating

They make great coffee, but even coming from the US the prices are steep. $15 for a cup of coffee. Come on.

I wanted to say the same about the pastries, but the baked goods in Copenhagen are on another level. Hart Bageri and Andersen were on repeat for me. I wish they stayed open later so I could have had them for dinner too. Some of these bakeries have several branches, so you don't have to make a trip out of it. Pick the one near whatever you're seeing that day.

<Photo
  src="/copenhagen/13-pastry.jpg"
  alt="A round pastry topped with cream and pink rhubarb on a white plate, lit by a patch of sun"
  caption="Breakfast, again."
/>

I'd recommend Torvehallerne, two glass market halls by Nørreport Station. It's a neat market with local food and other things: fruit, flowers, coffee.

Broens Street Food is the other place I'd send people. There are a lot of great stalls, and people eat outside or sit by the water eating, swimming, reading. Separately, I have to highlight Gasoline Grill. Either I was very hungry or they make the best burger I've ever had. The line is long, and it's worth it.

## Nyhavn

The city is effortlessly aesthetic. Things are in the right place at the right time, and the colors all seem to match. Same with how people dress: elevated, effortless, good-looking. Nyhavn is where that peaks. I could have stayed there a full day, just people watching.
