This trip started as a passing conversation. I was in Pittsburgh for Thanksgiving, hanging out with Ravesh and a few of our friends from CMU, and somewhere in there Belize came up. Ravesh is from there. A few months later I was flying down to spend Christmas and New Year's with him.

I call him the Prince of Belize, because as far as I can tell he knows half the people in the country. This trip would have been completely different without him.

## Christmas in Belize City

Belize is tropical, and a lot hotter and more humid than Pittsburgh. Ravesh picked me up at the airport in Belize City, and my first thought on the drive was that it felt like Indonesia. His family lives in the city. We stopped by his aunt's house, I met his mom, and I got a Christmas meal out of it.

Most Belizeans celebrate Christmas, and most things close on the 25th and the 26th. Plan around those two days.

Prices are mostly similar to the US, but a Belize dollar is worth half a US dollar, so my holiday brain treated everything as 50% off.

## San Ignacio

## Placencia

Then we drove down to Placencia, on the coast. It's more touristy than San Ignacio. My guess is that most people come to Belize for the beaches. There are some cool coffee shops and some Western food. Touristy also means nicer hotels, and we stayed in one.

## New Year's Eve

For New Year's Eve we figured the more the merrier, so we recruited two Brits we'd met, Lauren and Alex, to go all out with us. They drink a lot. Drinks with breakfast was a new one for me.

<Photo
  src="/belize/14-evening.jpg"

Of the two towns, I liked San Ignacio better. Downtown is small, and in the evening you can grab a Belikin and some okay pizza and call it a night.
